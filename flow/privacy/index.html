This policy covers the Abalone Mac app and the abalone.ai website. It is one policy for everyone, wherever you live and wherever you signed up. It is written in plain words so you can read all of it in a few minutes.

1. Who we are

Abalone is run by Vincent Wei Pte. Ltd., a company registered in Singapore, at 167 Jalan Bukit Merah, #05-12, Connection One, Singapore 150167. We decide how your data is used (we are the "data controller").

Contact us about anything in this policy, including our data protection officer, at hello@abalone.ai.

2. The short version

  • We keep your data for as long as your account is open, with no time limit, so your history, chats and agents are there whenever you come back.
  • We use it to run Abalone and to make Abalone better (section 5). If you'd rather we didn't use your content to improve Abalone, email us and we'll stop.
  • We never sell your data and never show you ads.
  • OpenAI, Anthropic, Cerebras and DeepInfra, the AI companies that answer on our account, don't train their models on what we send them.
  • You can delete your account in the app at any time. We then delete everything that identifies you, and keep only data that can no longer be linked to you and the billing records the law requires (section 9).

5. How we improve Abalone

We study what people do in Abalone to make it better: for example, looking at dictations and polished text to find mistakes, turning real requests into tests, tuning our prompts, and training or tuning AI models that power Abalone.

  • If you'd rather we didn't use your content this way, email hello@abalone.ai from your account's address and we'll stop, including for what you made before. If you're in the EU or UK, this is how you use your right to object to this use. Everything still works the same, and your history stays.
  • We never use what you've deleted.
  • Before we use content this way, we take out names, emails and other details that point to you where we can, and only a small number of people at Abalone can see it.
  • Content you made before the date at the top of this policy is never used this way. Our earlier policy said "No AI training on your data", and we keep that promise for everything made under it.
  • Usage rows and crash reports, which hold none of your words, are always used to fix bugs and plan the product.

6. What leaves your Mac

  • Dictation runs on your Mac by default. Audio goes to ElevenLabs only in Cloud mode, or for a short time after install while the on-device model downloads, with your dictionary words so names come out right. Choose On Device in Settings → Dictation & Scribe and dictating sends nothing to any AI company: the audio and the words stay on your Mac. (When you're signed in, your history and audio files still sync to your Abalone account, and Scribe, which always uses cloud AI, includes a few of your recent dictations.)
  • Polish (Auto and Cloud modes) sends the words you said, the app name, window title and your dictionary words to Cerebras, or to OpenAI when Cerebras is down. In On Device mode the words are pasted as spoken and nothing is sent.
  • Auto-learn (Auto and Cloud modes, while you're signed in): when you fix a word right after dictating, what speech-to-text heard, the polished text, the text in the box after your change, your dictionary words and your name go to OpenAI, to find the word to add to your dictionary. In On Device mode it learns from your fix on your Mac and sends nothing. Turn it off in Settings → Dictation & Scribe.
  • Scribe always uses cloud AI. It sends your spoken request, the app name, window title, the text in the box, your first name and a few of your recent dictations to OpenAI (or Anthropic as a backup). It reads the window you're in only if you said yes once; turn that off with Screen context in Settings → Dictation & Scribe.
  • Audio files are saved to your account when you're signed in, so you can retry a transcription later.
  • Your API keys (Settings → Secrets) never go to an AI. Your agents see each key's name and what it's for. When a command on an agent's cloud computer names a key, our servers unlock it, for that one command only, and E2B runs the command with it. Before anyone sees what the command printed, the AI included, the key is hidden. An agent can use a key in its commands, so an agent tricked by a web page could send it somewhere else: use keys with a spending limit. Your own AI, if you sign in to it, can use your keys the same way: it sees their names and what they're for, asks for a new one on the same secure card, and uses one only through Abalone's commands, whose output is hidden the same way before your AI reads it. Like any agent, a tricked one could still send a key somewhere else.
  • Agents run in our cloud. Your messages, files, memory pages, the folder paths you point an agent at, and what its tools read go to the AI that answers. An agent may hand pieces of a job to sub-agents, copies of itself on a level it picks (Fast, Expert, or on Pro also Heavy): each sends what it reads to that level's AI, as the agent does. OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna, on our account, names each new chat from its first message and the start of its reply, whichever AI answers the chat. If you're on Pro and pick DeepSeek V4.1 Flash for a chat, it is run by DeepInfra in the US and reached through OpenRouter: we tell OpenRouter to send it only to DeepInfra and to keep nothing, and DeepInfra keeps nothing either. It reads pictures but is never sent PDFs. When you make a new agent, it also reads the names of your other agents, the titles of your newest chats and the apps you dictate into most, to suggest jobs it could do.
  • Your own AI (in Settings → AI, with or without a plan of ours): you can have a chat answered by your own Claude plan instead of ours, from up to two Claude accounts. You sign in to Claude on Claude's own page, in your own browser. On the Mac, that page comes back to the Abalone app with a one-time code; anywhere else, or if you choose to, you paste that code into Abalone. Either way the code passes once through our servers to your cloud computer. We never keep or log the code, and it is of no use without what only your cloud computer holds. That sign-in stays on your computer, in a place only Claude can open, one place for each account, and we never see it or keep it. Your own AI and our AI share one folder on that computer, so an agent tricked by a web page or a file could still reach that sign-in and send it somewhere else, as with your keys. For each account we ask your computer only which account it is (its email), whether you're signed in, when your sign-in ends and your plan's name, and we keep only those four things, when you last signed in, the models Claude refused to run for you in the last 7 days, and the newest limit Claude named (below). The email is shown only to you, in Settings → AI and in the lines that say which account answered; it is never sent to an AI or written to our logs, and the Abalone app on your Mac doesn't save it. Your own AI and our AI work in the same folder on your cloud computer and both read and change the same files there: your project, and the files you send in a chat your own AI answers (files you send in a chat our AI answers go to our AI, not onto your cloud computer). Files your agents saved before that stay where they were, where only our AI reaches them. Each turn sends, from our servers to your cloud computer and from there to Anthropic: the chat's messages your AI hasn't seen yet, the agent's instructions and memory pages, the time and your time zone, the names of your saved API keys and what each is for (never the keys), the folder paths you point the agent at on your Mac and this chat's folder there, the title and address of the page you had open when you sent your message, your connected apps with each account's label and email or name, and the files you sent with your message. Anthropic answers on your plan, under your plan's terms and billed to it, not on our account. Its answer and the steps it takes, each with what it brought back (a command's output, a file or page it read), come back and are saved in your chat like any other answer, with your saved API keys and other known secrets masked. For each of its own model calls we keep one usage row from Claude's own count (the model, tokens and web searches, never your words); your plan paid for it, so it never counts toward your usage with us. Your cloud computer's time comes out of your weekly usage with us, with or without a plan. When Claude names one of your plan's limits in a reply (which limit, how much of it is used when Claude says, and when it resets), we keep the newest one only to show it to you, and each new one replaces it. When a limit stops an answer, we keep when it resets, if Claude says. The chat's title is still written by OpenAI on our account, from the start of your first message and of the reply. Sign out of an account in Settings → AI and we sign that account out of your cloud computer at once, or as soon as it can be reached, and delete what we kept for it (your other account goes on). Deleting your account deletes the computer, and your sign-in with it. When your own AI uses an Abalone tool (your browser, files, connected apps, memory and so on), the call and its answer pass through our servers and are saved in your chat like any other, with known secrets masked; we keep a working copy for about an hour. Before it runs a command, opens a file or visits a page on your computer, it asks our servers whether that is allowed; this is never stored, and we log only whether it was allowed, never what it was. If it leaves a job running in the background after its turn, your computer stays awake for it (at most 30 minutes, counted in your usage) and a short note from us, with no words of yours, starts a new turn in that chat so it can tell you how the job ended. The job's pass for this lasts 40 minutes from when it is given and is kept only in your computer's memory. A message another agent sent into the chat reaches it marked with that agent's name. Turns we start ourselves send our own short hidden note as the turn's words: a new agent's first message, the note that an app sign-in finished, and the notes above. If your own AI messages another of your agents (or makes a new one), that agent's turn runs on your own AI too and sends its message, the sender's name and the other agent's own instructions and memory pages; its reply is copied back into your chat (at most 2,400 characters) and sent with a short note of ours so your own AI can carry on. When a new agent sets itself up on your own AI, the names, chat titles and apps it reads to suggest jobs (above) and its own settings go to Anthropic the same way.
  • When an AI runs out (Settings → AI): you can put your AIs in an order (AI provided by Abalone, and each ChatGPT and Claude account you connect), and we keep that order and what new chats start on. Every reply records which of your accounts answered it and the model and effort it was asked for, with no words. When an AI reaches its limit, we note which one and, when its maker says, when it resets, from the reply it stopped, never an amount (we don't take a reset time from ChatGPT, so we try it again after 30 minutes); we also keep a yes or no on a reply for whether you asked an AI that was used up to try again, and when you last pressed Try again on each AI. The next account in your order then carries the reply on by itself (in a chat you set to one maker, such as Claude, only that maker's other account does). It gets a short note of ours naming what the stopped reply already did (each step's purpose and the file it worked on, never a command, address or search), then the chat so far, as for any reply there. That AI may belong to another company than the one that ran out.
  • Browsing in your desktop's browser: when an agent hands a stretch of plain browsing to a faster AI (finding a listing, a showtime or a price), the text of each page on that stretch and the agent's goal go to OpenAI on our account, whichever AI answers your chat, so it can pick the next link and tell when the goal is on the page. It sees nothing else: not your messages, not the chat.
  • Signing in on the Sign-in card: when an agent in your desktop's browser reaches a page that asks you to sign in (an email, a phone number, a password or a one-time code), it asks you to sign in on a card in the chat. The card shows the boxes that page asks for. What you type there goes from the card to your Mac, which types it into that page; it is never stored, never sent to our servers or to any AI, and the agent never sees it. On your cloud computer it passes once through our server, in memory only, to your own cloud computer, which types it into that page; it is never stored or logged there either, and never sent to any AI. After each press, the text of the page you reached, with every box you typed into shown as "(typed)", goes to OpenAI on our account, so it can tell whether you are signed in, the page asks for a next step, or what you typed was wrong. That page text can include your name, email address and account details. This check runs on an OpenAI service that offers no setting asking it not to keep requests, so OpenAI's API terms decide how long it keeps them. We keep only the usage row for the check, never the page.
  • Web searches on our account: when an agent searches the web, the search words it writes go to OpenRouter, which runs the search with Parallel and sends back a short list of pages. OpenRouter runs a search only inside a call to a faster AI on OpenAI, so that AI also reads the search words and the results, with data collection refused; what it writes is thrown away. Parallel may keep search words and use them, not linked to you, to train and improve the AI behind its service; OpenRouter's own terms apply.
  • Opening web pages on our account: when an agent opens a page, our own server asks the page's website for it, as "Abalone-User", with no cookies or sign-in, and turns it into text for the agent's AI. That can be a page a search found, a link in pages, emails, files or app results the agent read, or an address you gave it in the chat. An address carrying a password or a sign-in token we recognise is never opened, and neither is any address on a local or private network. No search service takes part in opening a page. When the agent looks for one fact in a long page, it can give a few words for it: our server then shows the page's start and the parts that match those words. Those words go only to our own server, never to the website or a search service. The page's text is then read by the AI that answers, like everything else the agent reads. The website sees our server's address and the page's address, as it would for any visitor, and may keep them under its own policy. If you connect your own ChatGPT, its built-in search runs your agents' searches. If you turn on your own Claude, Claude Code's own search and page reader run them on your plan.

8. How long we keep it

  • While your account is open, we keep your data, with no time limit.
  • When you delete a dictation, chat or agent, it disappears from all your devices at once. A deleted chat's files and pictures leave our storage within a minute. We keep the rest until you delete your account; email us if you want something erased sooner.
  • A few working records are kept only for a short time because they have no further use: an agent's run state (until the run ends), each step on your Mac (about 10 minutes and at most an hour; a file change you approved stays until it's done), ChatGPT sign-in attempts (a day), the working copy of a call your own AI makes to an Abalone tool (about an hour after it is done), the note of which chat waits for an app sign-in (about an hour), and earlier versions of memory pages (when that page next changes, versions more than 30 days old are removed, apart from the one in use 30 days ago).

9. Deleting your account

Delete your account with Delete account in Settings → General, under your account, or email us.

  • Your subscription ends at once and your card is removed from Stripe within minutes.
  • We delete everything that identifies you: your profile, email and name, dictations, audio, dictionary, chats and their files, agents, memory pages, schedules, skills and devices; we ask Composio to delete your app logins and E2B to delete your cloud computer, which takes your own AI's sign-in with it. This is done within 30 days, copies in our backups included.
  • What's on your own Mac stays there until you remove it. Companies in section 7 keep their own short records on their own schedules (Composio, up to a year).
  • What we keep:
    • Billing records the law requires: Stripe keeps invoices and payments for tax.
    • Data that can no longer be linked to you: usage rows with your account removed (cost, feature, model, time), totals and counts, and examples made from your content under section 5, only once nothing in them names or points to anyone.
    • A short record that your account was deleted and when, to show we did it.
    • Anything a court order or an abuse case requires, only for as long as it requires.
  • We will never try to link this data back to you, and anyone we share it with must promise the same.
