Polishing your dictation. In Auto and Cloud modes, your finished dictation text is sent through our server to Cerebras (its gpt-oss model) to tidy the wording before it's pasted, along with the name of the app you're in, its window title and your dictionary words. If Cerebras is down or slow to answer, our server sends the same request to OpenAI (its GPT-6 Luna model) instead. We don't keep the text beyond the usage record described above. Cerebras and OpenAI don't use it to train their models under their API terms, and may keep it only for a limited time for safety checks. In On Device mode nothing is sent: the words are pasted as spoken.

Scribe

Scribe is a separate shortcut from dictation: hold its key, say what you want written — a reply, a message, anything — and a draft appears, ready to insert into whatever app you're in. Your spoken request goes through our server to OpenAI, whose GPT-6 Luna model writes the draft; if OpenAI is down or slow to answer, our server sends the same request to Anthropic (its Claude Haiku model) instead. Every request also carries the name of the app you're in, its window title, any text already in the box you're writing in, and your first name (as your account, or else your Mac, knows it) so a draft fits the place and can sign off as you. So the draft sounds like you, our server also adds up to four of your own recent dictations from your synced history: the newest ones from the same app (or from other apps when it has fewer than two), never earlier Scribe drafts. This holds in every Dictation mode, On Device included: Scribe always writes with cloud AI. The first time Scribe would also read the window you're in (so it can see the conversation you're replying to), it asks first — a one-time question, not a question every time. If you say yes, up to about 2,300 characters of that window's text, around where you're typing, travel with your request from then on (our server passes on the end of the conversation and the names and unusual words in it), and you can turn it back off any time in Settings → Dictation & Scribe. None of this is stored by Abalone. OpenAI and Anthropic don't use it to train their models under their API terms, and may keep it only for a limited time for safety checks. A Scribe draft you actually insert is saved to your dictation history exactly like a regular dictation, so you can find it again later; a draft you never insert is never saved.

Agent data

If you use the agent and you're signed in, we also store your agent conversations (their titles and the transcript content of each turn — which can include excerpts of files the agent read for you, and the model's own summary of what it was thinking while it worked, which the transcript keeps so you can reread it later) and your project shells (a project's name, icon, and any standing instructions you write) in our database, so they sync across the devices you sign into. Agent turns run in our cloud, and our servers (not the app) write each conversation into your account. A new chat is named by the same AI that answers it (your own ChatGPT, or OpenAI or Anthropic on our account): its first message and the start of the agent's reply go to that model, and we keep only the title it returns. Images and files you attach to a message (up to 10 per message, up to 20 MB each) are stored in your account's cloud storage with that message, and are sent to the AI that answers — your own ChatGPT if you connected it, otherwise OpenAI on our account, or Anthropic on our account when the model is a Claude — while it answers that message, so it can read them. Deleting a conversation or project in the app soft-deletes it on our side too, and removes the files in it from our cloud storage within a minute: the images and files you sent in it and the pictures the agent saved in it (the conversation's hidden records stay until you delete your account). To show which chats have something new for you on every device, we also keep one number per chat: how far you have read it (the position of the newest message you've seen). It holds no words and no time. It stays with the chat and is removed when the chat is permanently removed or your account is deleted.

When the agent browses for you, each browsing step in that transcript also records the address of the page it reached, so you can click that step later — after quitting the app, or on another device — and get the page back. We take the credentials out of the address first: sign-in details in the address itself are dropped, and parameters that carry a password, token, or sign-in code are replaced with the word redacted. Search terms and filters are kept, because without them the address is a different page. Deleting the conversation deletes these along with the rest of its transcript.

When the agent wants to show you something it found — part of a page in its browser on your Mac, or its cloud computer's screen — it takes a picture of it and places it in its answer. We store that picture in your account's cloud storage with the conversation (up to 512 KB each), with a record of its size, where it came from and the step that took it, so the answer shows it on every device you sign into, with a link back to the page. Only pictures the agent takes to show you are saved. From its browser on your Mac, nothing is saved of a page with card fields, a typed password, or an app asking for access to one of your accounts, or of a page that couldn't be checked. Its cloud computer's screen has no such check: there, the agent is told never to show a password, card number or sign-in code. The AI that answers already sees each picture the agent takes, as it does today. Deleting the conversation removes its pictures within a minute.

Before an agent's tool results are added to the chat or sent to the AI, text in the shape of a known password, key or sign-in token is hidden. A key of an unknown kind, or one shown in a picture, can still get through. The raw result of a tool run on your desktop is kept on our server for about 10 minutes (never more than an hour) while the agent picks it up.

When you send a message while a page is open in the chat's browser, the message also records which page was in front of you: its title and its address, without sign-in details or anything after ? or # (so search terms are left out). Never the page's text. It is kept with your message and sent to the AI that answers with it, so “this page” means the page you were looking at.

Pointing an agent at folders on your Mac never uploads the files in them. The Abalone app tells our servers where those folders are — their paths, plus the names of the agent's first 20 source files, the descriptions you wrote for them, and how many there are — so the agent knows where to look. When a run can open files on that Mac, those paths go to the AI provider as part of the agent's instructions for that run. The files stay on your Mac; when the agent opens one there, what it reads is sent to the AI provider like the rest of the conversation.

When you make a new agent, it starts at once by asking what you'd like it to do, with a few jobs it could do for you to pick from or your own words, and sets itself up from your answer. To make those jobs and its setup fit you, its first replies (until it has a job or instructions) read the names of your other agents and the first line of what each does, the titles of your 10 newest chats, and the names of the 5 apps you dictated into most in the last 90 days, with how many times you dictated into each, beside the names of the apps you've connected and your saved notes, as every agent's replies do. Only names and titles (a chat's title is its own short summary): nothing inside your apps, and no messages from your chats or text from your dictations. They go to the AI that answers as part of that reply's notes.

When you add or reconnect an app from a chat, we note which chat asked, for up to an hour, so that when the sign-in finishes the agent there picks up where it left off. The note is deleted as soon as it's used, and a job deletes any note older than an hour every few minutes.

When one of your agents makes another agent, or asks one of your agents to change something, its words (the brief) open that agent's chat, labelled with the agent that sent them, and are stored in the chat like any message.

Memory

Your agents keep two short memory pages: each agent's own MEMORY.md (what it learned about working with you) and your USER.md (facts about you, which all your agents read). An agent saves a line while it answers you, and every save shows as a step in the chat. You can read and edit both pages in an agent's Identity tab. Each line is kept with the date it was added or last changed (the app adds the date to lines you type), so an agent can tell an old fact from a new one; the app doesn't show the dates.

  • Both pages go, as one note, to the AI that answers each run of an agent (your own ChatGPT if you connected it, or the next AI if a run fails over to it; otherwise OpenAI on our account, or Anthropic on our account when the model is a Claude), so it can use them. A run keeps the pages as they were when it started (in a long run, when it last summarized the conversation) until it ends, also while it waits for you on a card. So a change, or a Forget, reaches the agent's next run, not one already under way.
  • We keep every earlier version of a page for 30 days (your own edits within 10 minutes of each other count as one), and History on the page lets you put one back.
  • “Forget everything” on a page deletes the page and all its History. The chat where a line was saved still shows that step and the agent's call, which both hold the line. Deleting that chat hides them; we keep them until you delete your account.
  • Deleting a chat does not remove a line already saved from it. To remove it, open the page and delete the line, or ask the agent to.
  • Deleting an agent deletes its MEMORY.md and that page's History. Lines it saved to your USER.md stay until you remove them. Deleting your account deletes all your memory pages and their History.

Files you send in a chat are also saved in that chat's own folder on your Mac (~/Abalone/Chats), so the agent can open them there. We note which of your Macs saved each file and under what name, so the agent is told the right place to look. Deleting the chat moves its folder to the Trash, and signing out or switching accounts moves all of these chat folders to the Trash.

Connecting your ChatGPT subscription

In Settings → AI models you can connect your own ChatGPT subscription. It's off unless you do, and connecting means signing in with ChatGPT in your browser — so signing in is the opt-in. Once connected, agent runs (including scheduled ones) can use your plan instead of ours. Without it, your agents run on our own accounts when you have a plan with us: OpenAI's for a GPT model, Anthropic's for a Claude model (see OpenAI and Anthropic under “Who we share with”).

Each agent keeps the choice you (or the agent, when you ask it) make for it: a level (Fast, Expert or Max) or one model, with its thinking level; a chat can keep its own choice too, and, on one model, whether its older messages are summarized once it gets long (the “Fold long chats” switch, on unless you turn it off). We store that choice with the agent or the chat, and your set of levels (and any model you put on a level) with your account. When you send a message we work out which model answers it and keep that on the run, with the one backup behind it, so “Next AI” can try it. Which account answers is decided for each message: your ChatGPT plan when your ChatGPT account offers that model, otherwise our own OpenAI account when you have a plan with us. A Claude model always runs on our own Anthropic account, so it needs a plan with us. When the chosen model can't run there, a level's backup answers; a model you picked by name answers alone. None of this holds any words you wrote.

Your connection's sign-in session is stored encrypted in our database (Supabase, in the US) so those runs can use it. The key that unlocks it is kept apart from the database, so a copy of the database alone can't open it. We never see your password, and disconnecting deletes the stored sign-in. Those runs send your messages to ChatGPT (OpenAI), billed to your own subscription, and so does naming a new chat: its first message and the start of the agent's reply go to the same ChatGPT model.

We also keep which ChatGPT models your connected plan offers (model names and context sizes, nothing about you or your account), so Settings and your agents don't ask ChatGPT for the list on every request. We ask again after 5 minutes, or as soon as you sign in again, and the list is deleted with your account.

Each call still shows up in your usage history: we record one row — which feature it was, which model answered, how many tokens it used, how long it took, whether it finished, and what the same call would have cost on our own OpenAI account, so we can compare the two — never the words you dictated or said to the agent. It costs you nothing against our weekly and 5-hour usage limits; ChatGPT bills your own subscription instead.

Connected apps

In Apps & skills → Apps you can add apps such as Gmail, Google Calendar, Notion or Slack, and more than one account of the same app. Every one of your Agents can use the apps you add. Your app logins are held by Composio, our connected-apps provider, not by us; we store none of them.

For each account you add we keep its email address or name, as the app reports it, and the name you give it, so you and your Agents can tell your accounts apart. Removing the account deletes both, and asks the app to drop Abalone's access too (unless another of your added apps uses the same sign-in).

When an Agent uses an app, what it asks for and what comes back pass through Composio, and the result is saved in that chat like any other tool result. Composio keeps each of these requests and results (an email's text, for example) for up to one year. An Agent reads from your apps freely. A message to other people — an email, a reply, a Slack message — is never sent by the Agent: it waits as a draft on a card in the chat, where you can read it, change its subject and text (and for an email who it goes to and, for a new email, which of your accounts for that app sends it), and press Send or Discard. Anything else that would create, change or delete something runs only after you press the action's own button (Create, Save, Delete and so on) on a card that shows exactly what will happen and from which account; Cancel skips just that one action. An email that goes only to that account's own address is sent without asking. An Agent never moves or spends money (an ad budget or bid included), trades, or changes a password, even with your OK, and in accounting, payments and advertising apps it only reads. Each app call adds one usage row (the tool's name and the app, never what was read).

Each draft is kept in your account — who it's to, the subject and text, the account it would go from, any changes you made to them when you pressed Send, and whether you sent it, cancelled it, or it was replaced (by a newer draft, Try again, or the next message you sent while it was the chat's only open draft) — so the chat and your Inbox show it on every Mac you sign into. The Agent is told who each recent draft was for (as you changed it) and whether you sent it, never the words. When you press Send, the message (with any changes you made) goes out through Composio as that account. If you press Send or Discard while offline, your Mac keeps that press, and any changes you typed, until it can deliver it. A draft can be sent for 7 days. Drafts you never sent or cancelled are deleted when you delete their chat or Agent; sent, cancelled and replaced drafts stay until you delete your account.

Skills

Your agents keep skills: the steps an agent follows for one kind of task (a name, a description, instructions and any files), and which of your agents use each one. You work with them by asking an agent in a chat. They are stored in your account so your agents can use them on every Mac you sign into. An agent can make a new skill at once (you can ask it to delete one any time); it changes or deletes a skill only after you press Change or Delete on a card. Turning a skill on or off for an agent happens at once. Deleting a skill removes it from every agent; a run already under way keeps the version it started with. A skill grants no access to tools, accounts, files or computers.

Paying for a plan

When you subscribe, you pay on Stripe's own page: your card number goes to Stripe and never reaches us. From Stripe we keep only what a plan needs: your Stripe customer id (made when you first open the payment page, so you only ever have one), your subscription id, which plan you're on, its status (paid, payment failed, cancelled), the current billing period, when it ends if you cancelled, and a plan change waiting for your renewal and its date, plus the ids of Stripe's notices to us so each is handled once. After a change you make in Abalone, we read your plan from Stripe and save it before we answer, under an id of our own. Each agent run also notes which plan you had when it started, so we can see how each plan is used. If two payment pages ever start a second plan, we cancel it at once and refund its payment in full. If a payment for your current period is refunded in full (for example by Stripe's Link), the plan ends at once. You change your plan in Abalone, which asks Stripe to make the change; your card and your invoices are managed on Stripe's page. When you open Usage & Billing, Abalone asks Stripe, for your own account only, for what that page shows and stores none of it: your next charge (its amount with tax, its currency and date), your last three payments (the plan, the date, the amount, whether it was refunded, and a link to Stripe's page for each), and, only while a payment has failed or your card expires within 30 days, your saved card's brand, last four digits and expiry. Stripe's answer also includes your card's billing details and the name, email and address on your invoices; we pass none of that on and keep none of it. If a payment fails and you add a new card there, Abalone asks Stripe to pay what's due with it right away; the card's number still stays with Stripe. Where Stripe sells the plan for us (Stripe Managed Payments), Stripe is the seller of record: it asks for your name and billing address to work out the tax, adds the tax and pays it, and handles refunds and disputes under its own terms. Your address stays with Stripe: we never store it or show it. When you delete your account, we delete your customer at Stripe within minutes: your subscription ends at once, with no further charges, and your saved card is removed. The invoices and payments Stripe must keep for tax and accounting stay with Stripe.

What we do not do

  • No advertising. No ad networks.
  • No selling, sharing, or licensing data to third parties.
  • No behavioral tracking, no fingerprinting beyond the hardware identifier above, no cookies beyond what's required to sign you in.
  • No AI training on your data.

How long we keep it

We keep your data for as long as your account exists.

Earlier versions of your memory pages are kept for 30 days. Older ones are deleted the next time that page changes, except the version that was in effect 30 days ago, so History always has the page as it stood then. Our weekly backup keeps each copy of the database for 28 days, so a page you forget, or an old version that leaves History, is gone from our backups within 29 days.

When you delete your account (or email us at the address above asking us to), we delete everything within 30 days. That includes your dictation text, audio files, device rows, and profile, in our backups too.

Who we share with

We don't share your data for marketing. We use a small number of subprocessors to run the service:

  • Supabase — database, file storage and the agent's cloud work, in the US
  • Cloudflare — keeps our weekly backup copy of the database and stored files (R2 storage). It is encrypted before it reaches Cloudflare, so Cloudflare can't read it, and a deleted account leaves it within the 30 days above
  • Google / Apple — OAuth sign-in (only — we don't share anything else with them)
  • Stripe — takes payments for plans: your card, billing details and invoices are held by Stripe; we pass it your account id so a subscription is matched to you
  • ElevenLabs — cloud transcription, only when your audio goes to the cloud (the audio and your dictionary words, not kept)
  • Cerebras — cleans up your dictation text (automatic polish), receiving the text to tidy with the app, window title and your dictionary words (transit only, not kept by us; not used for training under Cerebras's API terms)
  • OpenAI — cleans up your dictation text only when Cerebras can't, receiving the same text to tidy (transit only, not kept by us; not used for training under OpenAI's API terms); and writes Scribe drafts, receiving the spoken request with what's listed under Scribe above: the app, window title, text in the box, your first name, a few of your recent dictations, and the window's text when you've allowed it (transit only, not kept by us; not used for training under OpenAI's API terms); and answers your agents when you have a plan with us and no connected ChatGPT: while a run works, it receives the conversation, its files, your memory pages and what the agent's tools read (web pages, Mac files it opens, connected-app results), and names a new chat from its first message and the start of the agent's reply (not kept by us beyond the chat itself and its title; not used for training under OpenAI's API terms)
  • Anthropic — writes a Scribe draft only when OpenAI can't, receiving the same request (transit only, not kept by us; not used for training under Anthropic's API terms); and answers your agents when the model is a Claude (one you or your agent picked, one of your levels runs, or our backup for one) and you have a plan with us: while a run works, it receives the conversation, its files, your memory pages and what the agent's tools read (web pages, Mac files it opens, connected-app results), runs the web searches the agent asks for, and names a new chat from its first message and the start of the agent's reply (not kept by us beyond the chat itself and its title; not used for training under Anthropic's API terms)
  • Epoch AI — once a day our server downloads its public ranking of AI models (the Epoch Capabilities Index) to show how capable each model is; nothing about you or your account is sent. The Smarts steps in the app are ranked by Abalone from Epoch AI's Epoch Capabilities Index, licensed under CC BY 4.0; we turn its scores into five steps
  • Composio — connects your apps (Gmail, Calendar, …): holds their logins, passes an Agent's app requests and results, and sends the messages you press Send on, only for apps you added, and keeps those requests and results for up to one year
  • E2B — runs the agent's cloud computer, only when one of your agents uses it: the pages it opens (and any sites you sign in to there), the commands it runs and the files it keeps are on a machine E2B hosts for your account
  • GitHub — hosts our app update releases (the update check sends your IP to GitHub's CDN, same as any download), and runs our weekly backup job: the database and stored files pass through GitHub's machines for the minutes the job runs, are encrypted before they leave, and are deleted when it ends
