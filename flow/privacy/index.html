Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-17

This is the privacy policy for Abalone Flow (the Mac app) and the abalone.ai website. It's written in plain English because we'd rather you read it than skim it.

Who we are

Abalone is a solo-founder project by Vincent Wei. Contact: vincent@vincentwei.co. For GDPR purposes, Abalone is the data controller for data collected through Abalone Flow and abalone.ai.

Web searches and page reads by the agent. When you ask the agent something that needs the web, your search query is sent through our server to our search provider (Serper.dev, which returns Google results — regular results, places, images, news, videos, or shopping, depending on what you asked). Pages the agent reads are downloaded through our server; if our own fetcher can't read a page (heavy JavaScript, scraping blocks), we fall back to a second service, Firecrawl, sending it just that page's URL. We don't save your queries or the pages you read to any database. To avoid paying for the same search twice, our server may hold recent search results in short-term memory for up to 15 minutes, then they're gone. What we keep is only a count of how many searches and fetches you made this month, which enforces the monthly included-usage cap (lawful basis: Contract — you asked the agent to look it up). The sites the agent visits see our server's requests, not your device.

We do not track your location, and we never see or store GPS coordinates; the app doesn't read your location. If you ask the agent something local (“coffee near me”), only a place you name in your own words goes into that search. We do save whether you've switched the agent's location permission on or off — just that yes/no, synced from your Mac — never a location. We also don't collect contact lists, browsing history, or anything from other apps. By default, Abalone transcribes your dictations on your Mac. Your audio goes to the cloud only if you choose Cloud mode in Settings, or for a short while after you first install Abalone, while the on-device model is still downloading. When it does, the audio streams as you speak through our server to our transcription provider, ElevenLabs (its Scribe Realtime model, which we ask not to keep it), is used only to produce your text, and isn't stored by us. Your custom dictionary words travel with it so names come out right. Prefer that audio never leaves your Mac, even during setup? Choose On Device mode in Settings — then dictation runs entirely on your Mac, works offline, and simply waits if the local model isn't finished downloading yet. On Device covers dictation only: Scribe always writes with cloud AI (see below). Audio is otherwise only uploaded to cloud storage if you're signed in, so you can retry transcription later from another device.

Polishing your dictation. In Auto and Cloud modes, your finished dictation text is sent through our server to Cerebras (its gpt-oss model) to tidy the wording before it's pasted, along with the name of the app you're in, its window title and your dictionary words. If Cerebras is down or slow to answer, our server sends the same request to OpenAI (its GPT-6 Luna model) instead. We don't keep the text beyond the usage record described above. Cerebras and OpenAI don't use it to train their models under their API terms, and may keep it only for a limited time for safety checks. In On Device mode nothing is sent: the words are pasted as spoken.

Scribe

Scribe is a separate shortcut from dictation: hold its key, say what you want written — a reply, a message, anything — and a draft appears, ready to insert into whatever app you're in. Your spoken request goes through our server to OpenAI, whose GPT-6 Luna model writes the draft; if OpenAI is down or slow to answer, our server sends the same request to Anthropic (its Claude Haiku model) instead. Every request also carries the name of the app you're in, its window title, any text already in the box you're writing in, and your first name (as your account, or else your Mac, knows it) so a draft fits the place and can sign off as you. So the draft sounds like you, our server also adds up to four of your own recent dictations from your synced history: the newest ones from the same app (or from other apps when it has fewer than two), never earlier Scribe drafts. This holds in every Dictation mode, On Device included: Scribe always writes with cloud AI. The first time Scribe would also read the window you're in (so it can see the conversation you're replying to), it asks first — a one-time question, not a question every time. If you say yes, up to about 2,300 characters of that window's text, around where you're typing, travel with your request from then on (our server passes on the end of the conversation and the names and unusual words in it), and you can turn it back off any time in Settings → Dictation & Scribe. None of this is stored by Abalone. OpenAI and Anthropic don't use it to train their models under their API terms, and may keep it only for a limited time for safety checks. A Scribe draft you actually insert is saved to your dictation history exactly like a regular dictation, so you can find it again later; a draft you never insert is never saved.

Agent data

If you use the agent and you're signed in, we also store your agent conversations (their titles and the transcript content of each turn — which can include excerpts of files the agent read for you, and the model's own summary of what it was thinking while it worked, which the transcript keeps so you can reread it later) and your project shells (a project's name, icon, and any standing instructions you write) in our database, so they sync across the devices you sign into. Agent turns run in our cloud, and our servers (not the app) write each conversation into your account. Images and files you attach to a message (up to 10 per message, up to 20 MB each) are stored in your account's cloud storage with that message, and are sent to the AI you chose (for example ChatGPT) while it answers that message, so it can read them. Deleting a conversation or project in the app soft-deletes it on our side too. To show which chats have something new for you on every device, we also keep one number per chat: how far you have read it (the position of the newest message you've seen). It holds no words and no time. It stays with the chat and is removed when the chat is permanently removed or your account is deleted.

When the agent browses for you, each browsing step in that transcript also records the address of the page it reached, so you can click that step later — after quitting the app, or on another Mac — and get the page back. We take the credentials out of the address first: sign-in details in the address itself are dropped, and parameters that carry a password, token, or sign-in code are replaced with the word redacted. Search terms and filters are kept, because without them the address is a different page. Deleting the conversation deletes these along with the rest of its transcript.

Pointing an agent at folders on your Mac never uploads the files in them. The Abalone app tells our servers where those folders are — their paths, plus the names of the agent's first 20 source files, the descriptions you wrote for them, and how many there are — so the agent knows where to look. When a run can open files on that Mac, those paths go to the AI provider as part of the agent's instructions for that run. The files stay on your Mac; when the agent opens one there, what it reads is sent to the AI provider like the rest of the conversation.

Files you send in a chat are also saved in that chat's own folder on your Mac (~/Abalone/Chats), so the agent can open them there. We note which of your Macs saved each file and under what name, so the agent is told the right place to look. Deleting the chat moves its folder to the Trash, and signing out or switching accounts moves all of these chat folders to the Trash.

Connecting your ChatGPT subscription

In Settings → AI providers you can connect your own ChatGPT subscription. It's off unless you do, and connecting means signing in with ChatGPT in your browser — so signing in is the opt-in. Once connected, agent runs (including scheduled ones) can use your plan instead of ours.

Your connection's sign-in session is stored encrypted in our database (Supabase, in the US) so those runs can use it. The key that unlocks it is kept apart from the database, so a copy of the database alone can't open it. We never see your password, and disconnecting deletes the stored sign-in. Those runs send your messages to ChatGPT (OpenAI), billed to your own subscription.

Each call still shows up in your usage history: we record one row — which feature it was, which model answered, how many tokens it used, how long it took — never the words you dictated or said to the agent. It costs you nothing against our monthly cap; ChatGPT bills your own subscription instead.

Connected apps

In Apps & skills → Apps you can add apps such as Gmail, Google Calendar, Notion or Slack, and more than one account of the same app. Every one of your Agents can use the apps you add. Your app logins are held by Composio, our connected-apps provider, not by us; we store none of them.

For each account you add we keep its email address or name, as the app reports it, and the name you give it, so you and your Agents can tell your accounts apart. Removing the account deletes both, and asks the app to drop Abalone's access too (unless another of your added apps uses the same sign-in).

When an Agent uses an app, what it asks for and what comes back pass through Composio, and the result is saved in that chat like any other tool result. Composio keeps each of these requests and results (an email's text, for example) for up to one year. An Agent reads from your apps freely. A message to other people — an email, a reply, a Slack message — is never sent by the Agent: it waits as a draft on a card in the chat, where you can read it, change its subject and text (and for an email who it goes to and, in Outlook, the address it goes from), and press Send or Discard. Anything else that would create, change or delete something runs only after you press the action's own button (Create, Save, Delete and so on) on a card that shows exactly what will happen and from which account; Cancel skips just that one action. An email that goes only to that account's own address is sent without asking. An Agent never moves or spends money (an ad budget or bid included), trades, or changes a password, even with your OK, and in accounting, payments and advertising apps it only reads. Each app call adds one usage row (the tool's name and the app, never what was read).

Each draft is kept in your account — who it's to, the subject and text, the account it would go from, any changes you made to them when you pressed Send, and whether you sent it, cancelled it or a newer draft replaced it — so the chat and your Inbox show it on every Mac you sign into. The Agent is told who each recent draft was for (as you changed it) and whether you sent it, never the words. When you press Send, the message (with any changes you made) goes out through Composio as that account. If you press Send or Discard while offline, your Mac keeps that press, and any changes you typed, until it can deliver it. A draft can be sent for 7 days. Drafts you never sent or cancelled are deleted when you delete their chat or Agent; sent, cancelled and replaced drafts stay until you delete your account.

Skills

In Marketplace → Skills you keep skills: the steps an agent follows for one kind of task (a name, a description, instructions and any files), and which of your agents use each one. They are stored in your account so your agents can use them on every Mac you sign into. An agent can make a new skill at once (you can delete it any time); it changes or deletes a skill only after you press Change or Delete on a card. Turning a skill on or off for an agent happens at once. Deleting a skill removes it from every agent; a run already under way keeps the version it started with. A skill grants no access to tools, accounts, files or computers.

What we do not do

  • No advertising. No ad networks.
  • No selling, sharing, or licensing data to third parties.
  • No behavioral tracking, no fingerprinting beyond the hardware identifier above, no cookies beyond what's required to sign you in.
  • No AI training on your data.

How long we keep it

We keep your data for as long as your account exists.

When you delete your account (or email us at the address above asking us to), we delete everything within 30 days. That includes your dictation text, audio files, device rows, and profile.

Who we share with

We don't share your data for marketing. We use a small number of subprocessors to run the service:

  • Supabase — database, file storage and the agent's cloud work, in the US
  • Google / Apple — OAuth sign-in (only — we don't share anything else with them)
  • ElevenLabs — cloud transcription, only when your audio goes to the cloud (the audio and your dictionary words, not kept)
  • Cerebras — cleans up your dictation text (automatic polish), receiving the text to tidy with the app, window title and your dictionary words (transit only, not kept by us; not used for training under Cerebras's API terms)
  • OpenAI — cleans up your dictation text only when Cerebras can't, receiving the same text to tidy (transit only, not kept by us; not used for training under OpenAI's API terms); and writes Scribe drafts, receiving the spoken request with what's listed under Scribe above: the app, window title, text in the box, your first name, a few of your recent dictations, and the window's text when you've allowed it (transit only, not kept by us; not used for training under OpenAI's API terms)
  • Anthropic — writes a Scribe draft only when OpenAI can't, receiving the same request (transit only, not kept by us; not used for training under Anthropic's API terms)
  • OpenRouter — names your agent chats: receives a chat's first message and the start of the agent's reply, and passes them only to US hosts that keep nothing (transit only, not kept by us)
  • Serper.dev — runs the agent's web searches (your query only)
  • Composio — connects your apps (Gmail, Calendar, …): holds their logins, passes an Agent's app requests and results, and sends the messages you press Send on, only for apps you added, and keeps those requests and results for up to one year
  • Firecrawl — fallback page-fetching when our own fetcher can't read a page (the URL only, and only when the fallback is used)
  • E2B — runs the agent's cloud computer, only when one of your agents uses it: the pages it opens (and any sites you sign in to there), the commands it runs and the files it keeps are on a machine E2B hosts for your account
  • GitHub — hosts our app update releases (the update check sends your IP to GitHub's CDN, same as any download)
